Summary of SOLID Principles in System Design

SRP: Ensures modules or classes have a single purpose, reducing complexity in large systems.
OCP: Allows systems to extend functionality (e.g., adding new services) without modifying existing code, crucial for scalability.
LSP: Guarantees that components can be swapped in polymorphic systems, maintaining correctness in plugin or modular architectures.
ISP: Promotes fine-grained interfaces, reducing unnecessary dependencies in distributed systems or APIs.
DIP: Facilitates loose coupling, enabling flexible and testable systems, such as in microservices or layered architectures.

By applying SOLID principles, you create systems that are easier to maintain, extend, and test,
which is vital for building robust, large-scale applications. Each principle addresses a specific
aspect of design, and together, they form a foundation for clean and maintainable code.
